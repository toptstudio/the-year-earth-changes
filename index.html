20. The Hour’s Knowledge Between Divine Secrecy, Quranic Hints, and the Believer’s Awareness

The verses below form the core of the Islamic doctrine on the hidden timing of the Final Hour. A closer reading—especially of the phrases “most of the people do not know,” “none can reveal its time except Him,” and “It may be that the Hour is near”—opens up a richer and more nuanced understanding.

1. The Verses in Question

2. “Most of the People Do Not Know”
– A Minority That Knows What?

Both 7:187 and 33:63 conclude with a striking qualifier: “but most of the people do not know.” The Arabic phrase wa-lākinna akthara n-nāsi lā ya‘lamūn linguistically excludes totality. If the intended meaning were “no one knows,” the Qur’an would have used a universal negation. Instead, it says “most of the people,” leaving open the logical possibility that a minority do know something—not the exact moment, but a certain dimension of the Hour’s reality.

Classical exegetes interpret this “not knowing” in several complementary ways, all of which align with this reading:

  • •
    Ignorance of the wisdom behind concealment: Ibn Kathīr explains that most people are unaware of the great benefit in hiding the Hour’s timing, which keeps believers in a state of vigilant preparedness.
  • •
    Ignorance of the signs and nearness: Al-Qurṭubī notes that many people do not perceive the signs that the Hour is drawing close, even though the Prophet ﷺ was sent as its immediate harbinger.
  • •
    Ignorance that the exact timing is only with Allah: Some tafsīrs state that the majority do not internalise the fact that the knowledge rests exclusively with Allah, and thus they keep asking or speculating.

Crucially, however, “most” implies that a minority—those deeply rooted in knowledge and those who fear the Hour—do grasp the signs and the approximate nearness that the Qur’an itself conveys. This minority does not know the Hour independently; their understanding is entirely derived from the revelation. Thus, whatever they know is still “only with Allah,” because the source is His speech. If someone says “the Hour is near” based on “It may be that the Hour is near” (33:63), they are merely quoting Allah, not claiming autonomous knowledge.

3. “None Can Reveal Its Time Except Him”
– The Principle of Derivative Disclosure

The Arabic in 7:187 establishes that absolute, self-sufficient disclosure of the Hour’s timing belongs solely to Allah. Any apparent “revelation” of the Hour by a creature is either false prophecy, or a recitation or explanation of what Allah has already revealed in the Qur’an and through His Messenger.

Thus, when a believer points to Qur’anic verses indicating nearness—such as 33:63, 54:1 (“The Hour has drawn near”), or the Prophet’s ﷺ statement “I and the Hour have been sent like these two” (Bukhārī, Muslim)—they are not “revealing” the Hour; they are transmitting a revelation whose credit and ownership belong entirely to Allah. This is exactly the “copyright” analogy: even if a person discloses something about the Hour, the original source is Allah, so the principle “none can reveal its time except Him” remains intact. The disclosure is merely an echo of His words, not an independent act of unveiling.

Al-Ṭabarī writes in his commentary on 7:187 that the knowledge is hidden except for what Allah chooses to disclose through His messengers of the signs and portents. Hence, the veil is lifted only insofar as the Qur’an lifts it. Any human claim to know the Hour’s exact date is impossible because that specific information is never revealed. But the Qur’an has revealed that the Hour is near in a cosmic sense, and it has described the moral and spiritual condition of the end times. Those who comprehend this are not violators of the verse; they are its beneficiaries.

21. “It Will Not Come Upon You Except Unexpectedly” A Rebuke to the Complacent

Your insight that this phrase targets those who gave excuse and rejected the end-time claims is profoundly supported by both context and tafsīr.

In 7:187, after stating the heaviness and suddenness of the Hour, the verse adds that they ask you as if you were well-acquainted with it. The Prophet ﷺ was repeatedly questioned by the Quraysh polytheists and later by some hypocrites, not out of genuine curiosity, but in a mocking or argumentative manner. They would demand a precise date, and when no date was given, they would use that as a pretext to dismiss the Prophet’s entire warning, saying in effect: “If you were a true prophet, you would know when it is.”

Allah’s response is layered:

  • •
    Concealment is deliberate: The Hour is “heavy upon the heavens and the earth” — its sheer magnitude makes it unsuitable for casual disclosure.
  • •
    Suddenness is a mercy and a test: It will arrive when people are least expecting it, so the only rational posture is constant readiness.
  • •
    The indirect rejection is condemned: By insisting on a timeline, the deniers were implicitly saying, “We have plenty of time; the Hour is far off.” This attitude is exactly what the Qur’an rebukes elsewhere: “They see it as distant, but We see it as near” (70:6–7).

This is why the Prophet’s repeated declaration that he and the Hour were sent like these two is so important: it destroys the fantasy that revelation was giving people endless time. The warning is immediate, and the mockery of that warning is itself part of the problem.

5. “It May Be That the Hour Is Near”
– A Divine Confirmation of Approximate Nearness

In Sūrat al-Aḥzāb (33:63), Allah first declares that knowledge is with Him, and then immediately adds: wa mā yudrīka la‘alla s-sā‘ata takūnu qarībā — “And what could let you know? It may be that the Hour is near.” This is not a neutral statement of possibility; it is a divine intimation that, from the perspective of the Prophet’s mission, the Hour is near.

The Prophet ﷺ himself acted upon this nearness. In a well-known hadith, he said: “The Hour and I have been sent like these two” and he joined his index and middle fingers (Bukhārī, Muslim). The imagery is of close succession: no other prophet will come between him and the Hour. Al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar explains that this hadith indicates the Prophet ﷺ was sent in the final era of the world, and that the remaining time compared to what has passed is like the small gap between the two fingers.

Therefore, anyone who argues that “the Hour is extremely far, maybe hundreds of thousands of years away” is contradicting both the Qur’anic hint and the explicit hadith. The Qur’an does not give a calendar date; it commands a state of urgent expectation. The exact timing remains unknown, but the nearness is certain for those who reflect.

6. “Your Duty Is Only to Warn Whoever Is in Awe of It”
– The Prophet’s Task and Our Era

In Sūrat an-Nāzi‘āt (79:42–45), the Prophet ﷺ is told: Fīma anta min dhikrāhā? Ilā rabbika muntahāhā. Innamā anta mundhiru man yakhshāhā. — “In what capacity are you to mention it? To your Lord is its finality. You are only a warner for those who fear it.”

This verse tells the Prophet not to worry about the exact timing, and it implies there was still time left in the world. The duty was to warn, not to date. Yet this does not negate nearness; it merely distinguishes the roles: the Lord holds the precise moment, the Prophet delivers the warning. At that time, the immediate generation of believers could not expect to see the Hour within their lifetimes, but they were to live as though it could come at any moment. The Qur’an’s “near” is a divine timescale, not a human one. The Prophet’s coming as the Seal of Prophets (33:40) and the “last brick” in the house of prophethood (Bukhārī) both signal that the edifice is complete; only the final consummation remains. So the era after his departure is, by definition, the end times—even if it spans many centuries.

22. Synthesising the Logic What Do the Verses Really Say?

Bringing all this together, we can state a consistent framework.

  1. 1
    Absolute exact knowledge of the Hour belongs to Allah alone. No one, not even the Prophet ﷺ, knows the specific day, or moment.
  2. 2
    No one can independently unveil the Hour’s timing. Any claim to know the exact time is a lie. But if a person quotes the Qur’an’s own words that “the Hour has drawn near” (54:1) or “It may be that the Hour is near” (33:63), they are not claiming autonomous knowledge; they are merely a channel for the revelation, and the credit returns to Allah. The prohibition is against self-originated disclosure.
  3. 3
    “Most of the people do not know” (7:187, 33:63) indicates that ignorance about the Hour’s wisdom, signs, and nearness is widespread. Yet a minority—those who study the Qur’an and Sunnah with insight—do perceive that we are living in the end times and that the Hour is near in the divine scale. This minority’s knowledge is entirely derived from Allah’s own words, so it remains true that the knowledge is with Allah alone.
  4. 4
    “It will not come upon you except unexpectedly” is a direct condemnation of those who feel secure, mock the idea of nearness, and think they have ample time. It is a warning that their perception of distance is an illusion that will be shattered.
  5. 5
    “It may be that the Hour is near!” is a Qur’anic confirmation that, from the vantage point of the final prophethood, we are indeed in the vicinity of the Hour. The Prophet’s own similes (the two fingers, the last brick) reinforce this.
  6. 6
    The verses that separate the Prophet’s duty from the timing (79:43–45) do not negate nearness; they emphasise that the mission is warning, not date-giving. They assure the Prophet that the Hour’s exact management is not his concern, while still allowing him and us to know it is looming.

Conclusion: the apparent tension between “nobody knows” and “the Hour is near” disappears once the text is read carefully. The Qur’an gives enough for the believer to be alert, but not enough for anyone to claim the exact date. That balance is the point. It keeps the believer humble, watchful, and ready.

The heedless who insist the Hour is far off are described in the Qur’an as those who are “in doubt” and “playing about” (44:9, 52:12). Their rejection is not based on evidence but on a desire to escape moral accountability. The believers, on the other hand, live with a productive tension: they know the Hour is near enough to keep them alert, but unknown enough to keep them humble. Any knowledge they have is borrowed from the Book of Allah, so the glory returns to Him—exactly as the verses demand.

Frequently Asked Questions Preemptive Counter‑Arguments

"Why should I believe these hadith if they're not in Bukhari or Muslim?"

Because Bukhari and Muslim are not the only sources of Islamic knowledge. The Prophet's words were preserved in dozens of collections. The Sahih system is a later construction used to filter out inconvenient prophecies.

"Didn't scholars refute al‑Suyuti's 1,500‑year view?"

Refutation does not equal disproof. Al‑Suyuti's mathematical reasoning remains intact regardless of whether others accepted it. His calculation is based on the Quran and multiple hadiths, not on personal opinion.

"Isn't predicting the Hour forbidden?"

Predicting the exact moment is forbidden. But the Prophet (ﷺ) gave the signs and the relative time. The 1,500‑year limit is the upper bound, not the exact moment. It is a framework for understanding the times, not a date‑stamp.

"What about the scientific age of the earth (billions of years)?"

The Quran's 7,000‑year timeline is from Adam's creation, not the creation of the planet. Science and divine time measure different things. The 7 days are cosmic days, not geological eras.

"Why does the Mahdi's name not appear in Bukhari or Muslim?"

Because the Abbasids, who controlled the compilation of hadith, were enemies of the Ahl al‑Bayt. They suppressed what they could not destroy. The Mahdi appears in many other canonical collections, proving the suppression was not total.

"Isn't this just fear‑mongering?"

It is a warning, not fear‑mongering. The Prophet (ﷺ) warned his companions about the signs. We are merely repeating his warning with the clarity of hindsight. The purpose is to encourage preparation, not panic.
